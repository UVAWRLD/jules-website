Vybe Together: Making plans feel spontaneous again

UX/UI · Professional · 2020-2024

Vybe Together helps people find and host small get-togethers nearby. A host posts a gathering, guests apply, and the host picks who comes. I co-founded it in 2020 and ran the brand and the app design. A lot of the nights further down this page sent their guests to RSVP in it.

Contribution: Creative direction, brand, UX/UI, launch strategy

Cape: Building a resort's identity from its market

Strategy · Professional · 2024

Before anything was designed, we needed to know who the resort was for. I went through the market data and landed on couples from the USA, Europe and the Middle East, mostly coming for wellness. Everything after, from the brand to the members' club, spa and restaurants, was built for them.

Contribution: Segmentation, market data, strategic analysis, brand identity

Mondadori Estate: Selling a Puglian estate to hotel groups

Strategy · Professional · 2024

Enzo

UX/UI · Independent

Enzo is a banking app I built with a membership system behind it. Your rank goes up as your wealth grows, and each rank has its own ring, from obsidian at $25K up to gold at $5MM. I designed the app and the rings, built the system around the ranks and wrote the marketing strategy, which uses the ranks as a sign of where you stand. People apply for access through a landing page.

Contribution: App design, membership system, marketing strategy

Rebranding Frozen Foods

Strategy · Academic

Four in five of the students I surveyed didn't like frozen meals, and none of them thought they were healthy. My first capstone asked what frozen food would have to change to match what people in cities now want from taste, health and sustainability. I did interviews, a focus group, a digital audit and desk research, then prototyped packaging for a range aimed at students.

The Surf Helmet

Product Design · Academic

Surfers get hurt in wipeouts, on the reef and on the sea bed, and hardly any of them wear a helmet. In Australia it's about 2%. The ones you can buy are uncomfortable and look wrong, and surfers care how they look. So I tried to design one they'd actually wear. The shapes come from animals, an armadillo and a jellyfish among them, and I took it through prototypes, an airflow simulation and the changes it would need to be manufactured.

The Mycelium Project

Product Design · Academic

We wanted to make everyday objects out of something that could be left outdoors and would feed the plants around it. Mycelium grows through farm waste like flour, cereal or sawdust, so we packed it into moulds and waited about a week for it to fill them. The first piece was an ashtray, then an incense holder. We also tested how the grown material held up to heat and fire.

The Hat Dilemma

Product Design · Academic

Most men's hats are a cap or a beanie. I wanted headwear with more character that still reads as menswear, so each design comes from one animal: a viper, a sparrow and a crow, a slug. The brief had nine rules to keep them wearable, like covering at least a quarter of the head, coming in two sizes and doing one job besides looking good. I worked from a target audience and a persona, through sketches and technical drawings, to renders.

The Toilet Project

Product Design · Academic

Why do toilets all look the same? I started from the most ordinary one, the exposed-trapway toilet, and used Raymond Loewy's MAYA rule (most advanced, yet acceptable) to judge how far it could change. The form takes long curves and horizontal lines from car design, drops anything that isn't needed, and mixes glass, concrete and steel in muted colours. I researched the object's history and anthropometry before drawing anything.
